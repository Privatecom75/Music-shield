What this does

  • Applies a slowly drifting micro-EQ wobble across the spectrum that alters spectrogram features without a fixed pattern, and that survives MP3/AAC re-encoding because it rides on the music itself.
  • Slowly rotates the phase of the track's own components (the same way on every channel), a change that is hard to hear but that lossy codecs pass through.
  • Adds pseudo-random noise shaped under a simple masking curve, so it hides behind the music instead of sounding like flat hiss.
  • Adds a very low-level component above 15 kHz when the sample rate allows; codecs remove this one, so nothing relies on it.
  • Runs entirely on this server with free, open tooling. No accounts, no uploads to third parties, originals are deleted after processing.
  • Returns a lossless WAV or FLAC so the perturbation is not smoothed away by re-encoding.

What this does not do

  • It is not AI-proof, not DRM, and not proof of ownership.
  • Re-encoding to MP3/AAC at 128–192 kbps keeps most of the change but the codec’s own error is larger than the perturbation; denoising, remixing, pitch-shifting, or resampling can weaken or remove it. Measurements are in METRICS.md.
  • A determined or adaptive attacker who knows the method can likely undo most of it.
  • We have not measured how much it degrades any specific AI model, and we will not invent a number.
  • Use it only on recordings you own or are allowed to modify. It is not a tool for handling other people’s music.

Full failure modes are listed in LIMITS.md in the repository.

Music Shield is an experiment in cheap friction. Keep your masters backed up separately; protected files are convenience copies, not replacements.
